Financial Services Landing Page Heuristic + A/B 2024

Marriott Bonvoy Amex — from a comparison table to a conversion machine.

A self-initiated redesign of a premium co-branded card acquisition page — grounded in heuristic evaluation, competitive benchmarking, usability testing, and a four-test A/B programme that projected a 47% lift in application click-through rate.

UX Designer — Audit, Wireframes & A/B Design

Financial Services · Co-brand Acquisition
01 — Objective

A premium card, undersold by its own page.

The Marriott Bonvoy Amex landing page drives applications for three co-branded cards ranging from $125 to $650 annual fee. Despite strong offer value and brand equity, the page buried its headline bonus below the fold, presented card comparison as a horizontal-scroll table on mobile, and obscured its trust signals behind footnote symbols.

Business goals

  • Increase application click-through rate (CTR) from the landing page.
  • Improve card-comparison clarity to reduce wrong-card abandonment.
  • Surface welcome-bonus offers earlier and more prominently.
  • Strengthen mobile conversion (est. 60%+ of inbound traffic).

User goals

  • Quickly understand which card is right for me.
  • Assess the welcome-bonus value without reading dense footnotes.
  • Feel confident I qualify before starting an application.
  • Compare cards on mobile without horizontal scrolling.
02 — Heuristic evaluation

Eight violations, four critical.

Evaluated against Nielsen Norman Group's 10 Usability Heuristics. Four issues were rated high severity — directly impacting comprehension and conversion.

HIGH

Aesthetic & minimalist design

The aspirational hotel hero image occupies ~40% of above-fold viewport, pushing all card offers and CTAs below the fold on standard mobile viewports (375px).

HIGH

Error prevention — bonus clarity

Two-tier bonus structure ("earn 100K then earn extra 50K") caused 5 of 6 usability participants to misread the total. Competitors show a single headline number.

HIGH

Recognition over recall

Horizontal comparison table requires users to scroll left/right on mobile to compare attributes — tested as completely unusable on 375px screens by all six participants.

HIGH

Visibility of system status

No pre-qualification eligibility signal or application time-estimate visible before the Apply Now CTA. Users can't assess commitment before clicking.

MED

Match: system & real world

"Elevated Offer" badge used on two cards; "Limited Time Offer" on the third — different language, same visual hierarchy. Creates confusion about urgency and priority.

MED

Help users recognize errors

The "Take the Quiz" module produces a card recommendation with no rationale — users don't understand why one card was chosen, reducing confidence in the result.

MED

User control & freedom

No clear return path from the quiz to the comparison table. Users who start the quiz and want to go back must use the browser's back button.

LOW

Flexibility & efficiency of use

No anchor links to specific cards. Users arriving from email campaigns targeting a specific card must scroll the full page to reach it.

Key finding: All four high-severity issues occur above the fold or within the primary comparison flow — the exact zone where conversion decisions are made. Fixing these four alone is projected to account for 80%+ of the total CTR lift.

06 — Redesign decisions

Six targeted changes, each mapped to a finding.

Hero: offer-first, not image-first

Move the total welcome-bonus value into the hero headline. Reduce hero image to 40% height. All three card names and primary CTAs above the fold on 375px mobile.

↑ Above-fold CTAs

Bonus clarity: one number, expandable detail

Collapse two-tier bonus into a single headline "150,000 Bonus Points" with tier breakdown behind an expandable "+See how" inline. Eliminates the most common comprehension error.

5/6 confusion → 1/6

Mobile: stacked cards replace H-scroll table

Vertically stacked card rows, each with an accordion "Key benefits" section. Sticky bottom bar allows "Compare selected" for up to 2 cards simultaneously.

6/6 usable on mobile

Quiz: hero placement, not mid-page module

Surface the card finder as a secondary hero CTA ("Not sure? Find your card →"). Quiz result now includes reasoning — users understand why a card was recommended.

Quiz start rate +41%

Trust: explicit two-line pre-qual statement

Replace the "Apply with confidence" badge with: "Check if you're approved — no impact on your credit score. Accepting the card may affect your score." Highest-trust variant in testing.

Submit rate +9.2%

Value calculator: fee as investment

Inline toggle showing estimated annual point value based on projected hotel spend (slider). Positioned directly below each card's annual fee to frame it as an investment, not a cost.

New: value framing

10 — Reflection & next steps

Removing friction is the product.

This redesign demonstrates that conversion improvement on a premium co-branded landing page doesn't require a visual overhaul — it requires removing the friction between a motivated visitor and the information they need to say yes. The product was already strong; the page was getting in the way.

Every change was grounded in an observable problem from the heuristic audit or usability sessions, then validated through a sequenced A/B test. That sequence matters: by testing decisions independently, each win is attributable and reproducible — not a black-box redesign that's impossible to learn from.

02Test a "fee justification" block immediately below each card's annual fee

05Legal/compliance review on pre-qual and credit-impact copy before live
