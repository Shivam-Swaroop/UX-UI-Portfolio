Premium Spirits Web Redesign Research-Led 2018

Jefferson's Bourbon — guiding every pour from curiosity to cart.

A self-initiated UX redesign blending heritage storytelling with modern usability — turning a beautiful but confusing bourbon site into a guided, conversion-friendly experience for newcomers, enthusiasts, and gift-buyers alike.

01 — The objective

Tradition meets a modern buying journey.

The redesign aimed to create a sleek, user-friendly platform that captivates visitors and enhances the bourbon-buying journey — integrating modern design trends and best-in-class UX principles while staying true to a 220-year family legacy.

Business goals

  • Improve discoverability of the bourbon collection — limited and essential lines.
  • Increase "Where to Buy" interactions and the add-to-cart experience.
  • Drive higher mobile engagement.

User goals

  • Quickly understand the difference between bottles.
  • Find flavor notes, aroma, awards, and popularity.
  • Easily find bottle availability — online or nearby.
  • Get help choosing the perfect bottle, for self or as a gift.
02 — About the brand

A legacy of craft and experimentation.

Jefferson's Bourbon is a premium spirit brand built on storytelling — founded in 1997 by Trey Zoeller and his father Chet, a noted bourbon historian. Together they continued a family whiskey tradition that traces back to Trey's 8th-generation grandmother, arrested in 1799 for the "production and sales of spirituous liquors."

With Jefferson's, Trey pushes the boundaries of bourbon — from ocean-aged barrels to experimental finishes — while honoring the craft that defines the category. The redesign needed to carry that same spirit of curiosity into the digital experience.

"Production and sales of spirituous liquors" — the family business since 1799.
03 — The problem

Beautiful visuals, but no clear path to a decision.

Premium spirits customers struggle to compare variants, understand flavor profiles, and find where to buy without friction. The original site relied heavily on visual storytelling but didn't provide a clear product-exploration journey or conversion cues — forcing users to rely on prior category knowledge just to choose and buy a bottle.

OPPORTUNITY 01

Guide product selection through a bourbon recommender — turning expertise into an interactive, friendly tool.

OPPORTUNITY 02

Improve the conversion journey through Where to Buy, store locator, and a smoother on-site buying experience.

OPPORTUNITY 03

Elevate the brand story with purposeful, scannable modules — without burying product information in copy.

Heuristic evaluation — selected findings

01

Recognition over recall — bottle differences require memory; no side-by-side comparison existed.

02

User control & freedom — the age-gate and modal flows blocked easy exploration.

03

Match with the real world — flavor notes and aging methods were buried in copy with few visual cues.

04

Aesthetic & minimalist design — beautiful visuals, but dense navigation and unclear hierarchy on mobile.

What people told us — guerrilla interviews (n=6)

I want to know quickly which bottle fits me.

Flavor notes and price range should be upfront.

Show me stores near me and whether it's in stock.

On mobile I just want three things: products, flavor info, and where to buy.

08 — Reflection & next steps

Craft and clarity don't have to compete.

This redesign balances premium brand storytelling with decision simplicity. By foregrounding a guided Finder, elevating product comparison, and clarifying buying paths, the experience becomes more educational, faster, and more conversion-friendly — without sacrificing the craft narrative that makes Jefferson's distinct.

If this were a live engagement, the next step would be validating these directions with real traffic and iterating on the parts users actually rely on most.
