Medela Pump & Care — a calmer companion for one-handed days.

A self-initiated redesign of Medela's companion app — replacing a cluttered, feature-dense dashboard with a focused daily loop: log a session, glance at status, and trust that everything's connected, all with one hand.

01 — The objective

A daily companion, not another app to manage.

The redesign set out to turn a feature-rich but overwhelming companion app into a calm, dependable part of a parent's routine — built around one-handed logging, gentle insights, and a visual language that feels supportive rather than clinical.

Business goals

  • Increase daily active use during active pumping/feeding phases.
  • Improve onboarding completion and pump-device pairing success.
  • Drive accessory reorders and product registration through the app.
  • Reduce support tickets related to sync and connectivity issues.

User goals

  • Log a pumping or feeding session in one hand, in seconds.
  • Understand milk supply trends without jargon or anxiety.
  • Get gentle, timely reminders that adapt to a changing schedule.
  • Share logs and schedules with a partner or caregiver.
02 — About the brand

Clinical credibility, everyday usability.

Medela is a Swiss-founded company with decades of history in breastfeeding and lactation support, known for research-backed breast pump technology used by parents and hospitals worldwide. The brand's positioning blends clinical credibility with everyday usability.

The companion app needed to reflect both sides of that promise — trustworthy enough to feel at home in a hospital setting, and simple enough for a sleep-deprived parent to use at 3am with one hand, without reading a single instruction.

Trustworthy enough for a NICU. Simple enough for 3am.
03 — The problem

Everything, all at once, when there's no time for any of it.

New and returning parents found it difficult to log sessions quickly while holding or feeding a baby, struggled to interpret supply trends without feeling anxious, and frequently lost confidence when Bluetooth pump-sync failed silently. The home dashboard tried to surface everything at once — schedules, tips, shop promotions, community posts — creating high cognitive load at exactly the moment users had the least bandwidth to process it.

OPPORTUNITY 01

Design true one-handed interaction patterns for logging, with minimal taps and large, thumb-reachable targets.

OPPORTUNITY 02

Make pump connectivity status always visible, with clear, calm recovery paths when sync fails.

OPPORTUNITY 03

Reframe supply data as gentle trends and ranges — not alarming single numbers out of context.

OPPORTUNITY 04

Introduce a shared "Care Circle" so logging and awareness aren't one person's sole responsibility.

04 — Process & methods

Research-led, accessibility-first.

The project reduced the app to its essential daily jobs first, then layered in supportive insights and sharing features once the core loop felt effortless.

Heuristic evaluation — selected findings

01

Visibility of system status — pump-sync status was buried in settings, so failed syncs went unnoticed for hours.

02

Error prevention — no confirmation step, leading to accidental duplicate or deleted entries while holding a baby.

03

Flexibility & efficiency — no shortcuts for repetitive actions; every session required the same multi-step flow.

04

Aesthetic & minimalist design — the dashboard surfaced ten-plus modules at once, competing for attention.

What parents told us — interviews (n=8)

I need to log this with one thumb while I'm holding her — I can't type anything.

I didn't realize the pump stopped syncing until I'd lost two days of data.

The chart made me think something was wrong, but it turned out to be totally normal.

My partner has no idea what's been logged unless I tell him directly.

05 — Users & personas

Three phases of the same journey.

Interview themes converged into three proto-personas representing distinct phases of the feeding journey — and very different contexts of use.

EP

The Exhausted New Parent

0–3 months · one-handed · low sleep
Goals
  • Log feeds and pumps with minimal effort
  • Build a routine without added stress
Pain points
  • No time or hands for multi-step flows
  • Anxious about "normal" supply numbers
Needs from the app
  • One-tap, one-handed quick log
  • Calm, reassuring trend language
RP

The Returning-to-Work Pumper

3–9 months · busy schedule · desktop & mobile
Goals
  • Fit pumping sessions around meetings
  • Track output to plan a freezer stash
Pain points
  • Pump-sync failures go unnoticed
  • Hard to plan around an unpredictable schedule
Needs from the app
  • Always-visible sync status
  • Smart, adaptive reminders
PC

The Partner / Co-Caregiver

Any stage · shared responsibility · mobile
Goals
  • Stay informed without asking
  • Take on logging during night shifts
Pain points
  • No visibility into existing logs
  • Feels like a guest in someone else's app
Needs from the app
  • Shared Care Circle access
  • Equal, role-based logging permissions

One-Handed Quick Log

Logging in under five seconds

A persistent, thumb-reachable card stack plus a home-screen widget lets a parent log a feed, pump, or diaper change in a single tap, with the time auto-filled. A gentle 3-second undo replaces the old confirmation dialog — fast, but forgiving.

Care Circle

Shared logging, shared visibility

Partners and caregivers get role-based access to the same live log — including night-shift entries. No more re-explaining what's already been done; everyone sees the same up-to-date picture.

08 — Reflection & next steps

Three jobs, done well, beat ten done halfway.

This redesign reframes the app around three things a tired parent can reliably do with one hand: log, glance, and trust that the pump is connected. By moving from a feature-dense dashboard to a focused daily loop — and reframing health data as calm, supportive trends — the experience aims to reduce anxiety while increasing the small daily interactions that build long-term engagement and trust in the Medela ecosystem.

If this were a live engagement, the next step would be validating these directions with real households and a clinical review of all supply-related language.
